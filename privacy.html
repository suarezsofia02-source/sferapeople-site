Privacy policy

Last updated: October 2026

Who we are

sfera is a hiring service operated by Sofía Suárez, an independent professional based in Buenos Aires, Argentina. For any question about your data, write to contact@sferapeople.com.

What we collect

  • From companies: your work email, the service you are interested in, the roles you are hiring for, and anything else you share in the contact form.
  • From candidates: name, email, LinkedIn profile, area, seniority, country, English level and your CV, if you upload it, plus the date you agreed to be contacted.
  • Basic technical data, such as IP address and browser type, which our hosting provider records to keep the site working and secure.

We do not ask for sensitive data such as ID numbers, health information, religion or political views. Please do not include them in your CV or messages.

Why we use it

To reply to requests from companies, to contact candidates about real openings that match their profile, and to keep a record of our work. We do not sell your data or use it for advertising.

Who can see it

sfera and the specialists who work with us on a search. We also use service providers to run the website and store records: Netlify (hosting and forms), Airtable (our records database), an automation service that moves form submissions into that database, and Google (email and call scheduling). These providers process data on our behalf and may store it outside your country, including in the United States.

We share a candidate profile with a hiring company only after talking with the candidate about that specific role.

How long we keep it

We keep candidate profiles for up to 24 months after our last contact, and company enquiries for as long as they are useful to our work together, or until you ask us to delete them.

Your rights

You can ask to access, correct or delete your data, or withdraw your consent, at any time by writing to contact@sferapeople.com. We will reply within a reasonable time.

In Argentina, personal data is protected by Law 25.326, and you can complain to the Agencia de Acceso a la Información Pública (AAIP). If you are in the European Union or the United Kingdom, you may also have rights under the GDPR, including contacting your local data protection authority.

Changes

If we change this policy, we will update the date at the top of this page.
