Salary data for LatAm engineers is scattered across reports, recruiter chats and guesses. In October 2026 we ran a short poll on LinkedIn to get a clearer picture, and 524 people voted. This article shares the results and, just as important, what they do not tell you.

## The question

"Senior engineer (6+ yrs) in LatAm: monthly pay in USD?" The post was aimed at senior engineers based in LatAm who work for a US company. We only report aggregated results.

## The results

| Monthly pay in USD | Share of votes |
|---|---|
| $3K to $5K | 20% |
| $5K to $7K | 52% |
| $8K to $10K | 20% |
| $10K+ | 7% |

LinkedIn rounds each percentage, so the total adds up to 99%.

## What stands out

- More than half of the votes (52%) landed in the $5K to $7K range, so the middle of the answers sits there.
- 72% of voters chose a range that tops out at $7K or below.
- 27% chose $8K or more, and 7% chose $10K or more.

## How to read this poll (please read this part)

This is a pulse check from our network, not a market benchmark. Before you quote or use any of these numbers, keep these limits in mind:

- **It is not a representative sample.** The 524 voters come from our own LinkedIn audience and chose to answer, and we cannot verify that every voter is a senior engineer working in LatAm for a US company.
- **The ranges have a flaw.** Two options share the $5K boundary, and there was no option for $7K to $8K. Anyone paid in that range had nowhere to vote.
- **It says nothing about country, stack or contract type.** A senior backend engineer in one country and a senior ML engineer in another can sit far apart, and the poll cannot tell them apart. It also does not separate contractors from employees.
- **It measures what people say they earn, not what companies offer today.**

## How to use it if you are hiring

Treat the poll as a conversation starter, not a salary band. A few practical ways to use it:

1. **Check your range against more than one source.** Compare this poll with other salary reports, your own past offers and what candidates tell you in screening calls.
2. **Define the role before the number.** Stack, scope, time zone overlap and the kind of contract change the price more than the word "senior".
3. **Say your range early.** In a smaller poll of 67 people in our network, 20% said that not sharing a salary range makes them drop out of a hiring process. It also saves everyone's time. More in [our hiring guide](/insights/how-to-hire-senior-engineers-in-latam).
4. **Ask, do not assume.** Ask candidates what setup and currency they expect. Our companion poll on contractor versus employee setups shows why that matters: [read the results](/insights/contractor-vs-employee-eor-latam-engineers).

## How to use it if you are a candidate

Use it as one reference point, not as proof of what you are worth. Your country, stack, years of hands-on experience and the contract type all move the number. Compare it with offers you have seen and with people in your own market.
