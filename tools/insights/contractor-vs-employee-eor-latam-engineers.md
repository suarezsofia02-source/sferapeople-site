When a US startup hires an engineer in Latin America, one of the first decisions is the setup: how will this person legally work with you? We asked our LinkedIn network what they prefer. 203 people answered.

## The question

"For a remote job at a US company, which setup do you prefer?" The post was aimed at LatAm engineers working for US companies. It also explained that an EOR or PEO is a third party that legally employs you on behalf of the US company.

## The results

| Setup | Share of votes |
|---|---|
| Contractor, paid in USD | 59% |
| Depends on the offer | 19% |
| Local employee, USD pay | 12% |
| Employee via EOR / PEO | 8% |

LinkedIn rounds each percentage, so the total adds up to 98%.

## What stands out

- About 6 in 10 voters prefer to be a contractor paid in USD.
- Only 8% chose an employee setup through an EOR or PEO.
- 19% said it depends on the offer. For them, the details of the package matter more than the label.

## The three setups in plain words

- **Contractor.** The engineer invoices your company and handles their own taxes and benefits. This is common for remote work, and it is also the setup where labor rules differ most from country to country.
- **Local employee.** The engineer is employed under local labor law, by a local entity. The pay can still be in USD in some cases.
- **EOR or PEO.** A third party becomes the legal employer in the engineer's country and manages payroll and compliance for you. You manage the work day to day.

## How to read this poll

This is a pulse check, not a market study. The 203 answers come from our own LinkedIn network and people chose to vote, so they cannot speak for every engineer in LatAm. We do not know the country, seniority or exact job title of each voter. Use it to understand preferences, not to decide your legal setup.

## What this means for founders

Most candidates in this poll lean toward being paid in USD as contractors, but preference is only one input. The right setup depends on the country where the person lives, how the work is organized and what your lawyer or accountant advises. Decide it with them before you start interviewing, and explain it clearly to candidates early. The 19% who answered "depends on the offer" are usually weighing the whole package: pay, stability, benefits and growth.

## A note on risk

Contractor versus employee has legal and tax consequences that depend on the country and on the facts of each case. Misclassifying a worker can lead to penalties and back payments. This article is general information, not legal or tax advice. Talk to a lawyer or accountant who knows the engineer's country and yours before you decide.
