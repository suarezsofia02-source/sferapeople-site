Founders hiring in Latin America usually ask the same questions. What do candidates expect to be paid? How do they want to be hired? What makes them walk away? And is it even a good moment to hire there?

Over October and November 2026 we ran four polls in our LinkedIn network, and we put the answers next to two public market reports. This article brings both views together: what candidates told us, what the market data says, and where they meet.

## The candidate view: four polls, 921 votes

| Poll | Votes |
|---|---|
| Monthly pay for a senior engineer in LATAM | 524 |
| Contractor, local employee or EOR | 203 |
| What matters most in a US startup job offer | 127 |
| What makes you drop out of a hiring process | 67 |

Some people may have voted in more than one poll. These are our own followers, who chose to vote, so this is a pulse check and not a market study.

### 1. Pay comes first

We asked what matters most in a job offer from a US startup. Of 127 votes:

| Option | Share of votes |
|---|---|
| Salary in USD | 58% |
| Growth and ownership | 22% |
| Stability and funding | 13% |
| Time zone overlap | 5% |

LinkedIn rounding makes the total 98%. Salary in USD led by a wide margin, and growth and ownership came second. Time zone overlap got the fewest votes, which makes sense for a group that already lives in similar time zones to the US.

### 2. The number most people picked

Of 524 votes on the monthly pay of a senior engineer (6+ years) in LATAM working for a US company, 52% chose $5K to $7K, 20% chose $3K to $5K, 20% chose $8K to $10K and 7% chose $10K or more. The ranges overlap at $5K and skip $7K to $8K, so read it as a rough shape, not a salary band. We explain the limits in [What senior engineers in LATAM earn per month](/insights/latam-senior-engineer-salary-poll).

### 3. The setup most people prefer

Of 203 votes, 59% prefer to be a contractor paid in USD, 19% said it depends on the offer, 12% prefer a local employee paid in USD and 8% prefer to be an employee through an EOR or PEO. The setup has legal and tax consequences that depend on the country, so preference is only one input. More in [Contractor, local employee or EOR](/insights/contractor-vs-employee-eor-latam-engineers).

### 4. What makes them walk away

Of 67 votes, 43% said 5+ interview rounds, 23% long take-home tests, 20% no salary range shared and 11% slow feedback (the total is 97% because of rounding). The sample is small, but the message is clear: process is part of the offer.

## The market view: demand is growing and spreading

Two public reports give context. Both come from a single source each, so treat them as signals.

- **Where US companies place talent in LATAM.** Hire With Near reported that in the first half of 2026 its placements were spread across several countries: Brazil 22%, Colombia 18%, Argentina 14%, Mexico 12% and El Salvador 5%. Its placements grew 67% compared with the first half of 2025. This is one platform's data, not the whole market. Source: [Hire With Near, Midyear Hiring Report](https://www.hirewithnear.com/midyear-hiring-report).
- **Remote work is mainstream for developers.** In the 2025 Stack Overflow Developer Survey, 32.4% of respondents said they work fully remote and 17.9% fully in person. The rest are hybrid or chose a flexible setup. Among US respondents, 45% were fully remote. Source: [Stack Overflow Developer Survey 2025](https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2025/work).

## Where the two views meet

This part is our reading, not a finding.

- **Competition for talent is not limited to one country.** If US demand is spread across several LATAM countries, candidates in each of them can have several options.
- **Pay clarity is the entry ticket.** Candidates put salary in USD first and 20% of our drop-out poll said a missing range makes them leave. Hiding the range costs you good candidates early.
- **Process is the part you control.** You cannot change the market, but you can run fewer rounds, shorter tests and faster feedback.
- **Growth and ownership matter to a fifth of candidates.** For many roles that is where a startup can beat a bigger company's offer.

## What founders can do with this

1. **Share your pay range in the first conversation.** Decide it before you post the role.
2. **Decide the setup with your lawyer or accountant early.** Then explain it clearly to candidates.
3. **Cut the process to what you need.** Use a scorecard, keep the rounds low and keep take-homes short.
4. **Answer fast.** A clear yes or no in days beats a perfect answer in weeks.
5. **Sell the growth and ownership.** Say what the person will own in six months.

## What candidates can take from it

Use the numbers as one reference, not as proof of what you are worth. Ask for the pay range and the setup early, so neither side loses time. Compare offers on the full package: pay, setup, growth, stability and how the company runs its process.

## Limits of this article

The polls come from our own network and are self-selected. We do not know each voter's country, stack or seniority. The market reports each come from one source. Nothing here is legal, tax or salary advice, and it is not a recommendation of what to pay.
